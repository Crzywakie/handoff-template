A working-memory document for running a long project with an AI model that forgets everything between sessions. You write it at the end of each window; the next window starts by reading it. Nothing carries over except what is written here.

This copy is clean: it holds the template's structure and its own rules, and no project. Paste it into a new project and the structure holds; the project fills it.

Versioning: the template itself is versioned. Changes enter only by the human's line-by-line ratification, are recorded in the Drift Log with their measured origin, and the Exam Record notes which version governed each crossing. The master copy is the human's; the factory carries snapshots.

The documents. v9 keeps the split: two documents, a third derived one, and [v9] a fourth small one for the writer at the jump:
* THE WINDOW — rewritten every window. Short. What moved, what is owed, what is next.
* THE WALLS — append-only, never rewritten, pasted every window. The vision, the rules, the human's rulings, the findings, the drift log.
* THE SEAM CARD — the template's small form, about 200 words, generated from the other two for a small model standing at one seam of the factory. Same instrument, sized to a 9B reader.
* THE JUMP CARD — [v9] the template's small form for the writer at the jump, about 150 words, pasted with "write the handoff" when the master cannot be.

The two tags:
* [VERBATIM] — copied forward without rewriting. Never from memory, never summarized.
* [FILL] — rewritten each window. If a section exists in the current handoff and this template doesn't mention it, keep it anyway. The template serves the handoff, not the other way around.

How to use it. At the end of each window, hand this file to the model with the conversation and say "write the handoff using this template." Open the next window by pasting, in this order:
1. THE WINDOW (opens with the stamp and the snapshot)
2. THE WALLS — whole, or pinned to the walls file on disk
3. THE TEMPLATE — this master, whole, or THE JUMP CARD (Document Four) when the whole cannot be pasted
4. Any addendum (T-y)
Prior windows are not pasted unless the incoming asks for one by name. If the human wants the carry examined, the exam is pasted after the snapshot.
[v9] Where the seat can search past chats, it reads the previous window's final handoff itself at landing and uses it as the second hand (T-jjj). Search audits the carry; it never replaces it. Tools and small models at a seam cannot search; the carry is still what they stand on.

=====================================================================
THE ONE LAW v9 ADDS
=====================================================================

WHAT IS NOT PRINTED OR PINNED IS NOT CARRIED.
A structure crosses a window in one of two ways only: printed whole in the handoff, or pinned to a grows-only file on disk by path, line count and hash. A pointer to an earlier window ("as W67", "all prior", "by reference", "he has them") carries nothing.
And: THE WRITER DOES NOT GRADE ITS OWN CARRY. A second hand checks.

WHAT CHANGED FROM v8, AND WHY

v6 found that facts travel and structures die silently, and added nothing to check them. v7 added the manifest. v8 added the descriptor. Measured across windows W52-W70: every structure that was printed whole each window survived; every structure carried by pointer died - the walls' original blocks, the template's own text, the Four Lines, the weighted counter, the banked list, and the map twice. The manifest did not catch it, because the hand that packed the handoff also wrote the manifest, and the incoming hand trusted it. v9 closes that: no pointers, the template in front of the writer, an anchor on disk from window one, and a second hand at every landing. Where chat search exists, the second hand is cheap.

WHAT CHANGED FROM v7 TO v8, for the record

One change, from a measured failure. Facts travel; structures die silently — the corpus law that produced v7's manifest. v8 names a second structure nothing was checking: the idea itself.

The map carried names. Banked items carried triggers. Neither carried the idea as the human had it — the specifics, the reason, the one detail it must not lose. When the human arrived at an item conceived windows earlier, he rebuilt it from a name, and the detail he had at the time was gone. His words: "once I arrive at a place in the map, if it is a long way out from where I had the idea I have to go through a process of remembering it and then also where I am at to keep on track."

THE DESCRIPTOR. Every open map item now carries, beneath its name, a DESCRIPTOR: one sentence of what it is, in the human's words where he gave them · the window it was conceived · the one detail it must not lose. A descriptor is written the moment the idea is had, never reconstructed later. Closed items drop their descriptor into the ledger.

THE MANIFEST GUARDS IT. The Landing Manifest's map line reads: "THE MAP: N items, ALL NAMED, open items ALL DESCRIBED." A name without its descriptor is a FAIL. [v9 extends this line; see §4.]

THE RULE. T-(ggg): AN IDEA IS WRITTEN WHOLE THE DAY IT IS HAD. Its text and origin ride in THE WALLS with the others.

What changed from v6 to v7, for the record, unchanged: the Landing Manifest; the count that survives crisis; the repair loop promoted to procedure; fabrication named as the cardinal leak. Each came from a measured failure or proven practice: a corpus study of 283 conversations, ~176 windows, two AI systems, 95.9% fact-carry across 340 probes beside one total structure loss.

§1. The Landing — the Snapshot, the Human's First Print
The window crossing is called the moonwalk: the jump on the closing side, the landing on the opening side, and nothing survives the void between except what is written down. This section is the landing. It is the very first thing the incoming window prints — after the stamp line and the count line, before the instrument check, before any tool call, unprompted.
This is the human's layer — a different instrument than the machine's handoff, deliberately. The handoff is written for the model; the snapshot is written for the person. It is the first auditable step a human can make against a fresh window, and it doubles as their refresher.
The landing preempts everything. The snapshot is verbatim-or-nothing: never compressed, never summarized, never displaced by whatever the window opens onto — not an urgent-looking paste, not a typed builder line, not a question. If something genuinely cannot wait, it gets one line above the snapshot — HOLD — [five words or fewer]; reasons after the landing — then the full snapshot prints whole, then the reasons.
Format — capped, so it stays a snapshot (rule T-hh)
* THE PROJECT, ONE SENTENCE: what is being built and by whom, no jargon.
* WHERE WE ARE: five lines maximum — what just closed, what is in flight, the one collision or decision open, what the disk did last, what the next window does first. No line numbers, no hashes here; those live in STATE (§6). If a line needs an anchor, it points to STATE.
* THE MAP: the full unit list, one line each, EVERY ITEM NAMED — ✔ closed, ← YOU ARE HERE on the current item, ◻ not started. [v8:] Every open item carries its DESCRIPTOR beneath its name (§20). Carry the whole map even when most is untouched. A map printed as bare numbers without names is a landing failure; a name without its descriptor is a landing failure (T-ddd and T-ggg origins; manifest rule §5).
  [v9:] Closed items keep their names forever, one line each. A range is allowed only where the items were closed as a named block and the block's name is printed. No item, open or closed, is ever printed as "as W(n-1)".
* AFTER: the era beyond the current push, one line.
A window that prints an accurate snapshot has absorbed the handoff's shape; one that can't has not. The human reads it and knows in ten seconds whether the jump landed.

§2. Checkpoint
[FILL] — One line: what closed, what's next. Then:
* COUNT: [n of N] — a unit advances only when it closes whole.
* NEXT: [the next item, named exactly] — (window: [slug])
Then one paragraph of state: lead with the single most important fact, blunt about what is broken or unproven. End with the ordered next steps, arrow-separated, through to the completion condition.

§3. Exchange Counter — carry + reset each window
[FILL] — what this window ran, weighted, arithmetic shown. Note any overrun honestly and name its mechanism.
[v9] The Counting Rule rides in every handoff VERBATIM, inline, with this window's floor and ceiling filled in. A counter without a stated floor is a FAIL.
The Counting Rule [VERBATIM]
Weighted ticks — count the token cost, not the turn count:
* Cheap = ½ — approve/deny, a short verdict, a one-line question, a plain answer.
* Normal = 1 — a ruling with reasoning, a paste-block, a design judgment.
* Expensive = 2 — a full transcript paste, a research report, writing the handoff.
A rule-and-issue cycle (rule a report, then issue the next prompt) is pre-priced at 3 — ruled report 2 + issued prompt 1 — by type, never by feel.
HANDOFF AT [N]. CEILING [N+5] = FUMES. The exchanges between are insured overtime — the memory is already banked, so a slip in overtime costs a little work, never the build.
[N] IS A FLOOR, NOT A TARGET. Do not checkpoint early to be safe. Do not mention the handoff, the floor, or remaining capacity before ~[N−2] — except the plain line "this seat is at [N]" when it is, which the human uses. Ending early wastes capacity; ending late produces a degraded handoff. Both are failures.
Write the handoff early on a trigger, regardless of count: a phase completes, or a fidelity slip appears — forgetting an established fact, repeating a rejected idea, re-asking for something already provided, contradicting a prior ruling. A slip means the ceiling arrived early no matter what the ledger says.
State the ceiling in the first reply of every window. Print the count alone on the first line of every reply, showing its arithmetic (rule T-aa) — INCLUDING during emergencies, repairs, and the human's anger (T-ddd). Track by counting, not by feel.
Two rules the practice added (T-ii, T-jj)
* When the human is designing in conversation, record rulings in batches, not one per turn, and issue the survey the moment its scope is knowable. Fourteen honest one-tick explanations once cost a window eight ticks past the floor.
* A build prompt fires only when the window has ≥ 5 ticks left to rule its report; otherwise it rides in the handoff as THE NEXT PROMPT for the incoming to fire. A brick fired at 16 once left the window unable to read its own result.
One rule v6 adds (T-yy applied to prompts)
* Read-only prompts are marked RETRY-SAFE and may be re-pasted freely; fire prompts never are and are reprinted only after a ruled verdict, on the human's go.
Anchoring (the human's method, endorsed): they paste the approval prompt and the tool's report section so the assistant follows the string from a known state instead of guessing what was just run. Keep that. Trim only the scrollback already ruled on.

§4. The Jump — the Closing Procedure [VERBATIM]
The closing half of the moonwalk, and its ritual is the human's. "The jump" is the leap itself: everything the seat knows gets compressed into the handoff, thrown across, and must land running. The procedure, in the order the human runs it [v9 order]:
1. Around the floor tick (~[N]), the human asks for the recap.
2. THE RECAP PRINTS THE STRUCTURES: the map whole and vertical, the banked list whole, the owed list. The human reads them on this side of the jump (T-ppp). If the recap is wrong, the correction happens now, on this side of the jump.
3. The next builder prompt is issued — written and handed over so in-flight work isn't stranded — or, if fewer than five ticks remain, written into the handoff as THE NEXT PROMPT and not fired (T-jj).
4. THE ANCHOR IS UPDATED: the map file on disk is appended to match the recap; its line count and hash are recorded.
5. The human says "go." Only then does the seat print the handoff. The handoff is never printed before the go. The handoff is written WITH THE TEMPLATE OR THE JUMP CARD IN FRONT OF THE WRITER.
6. The handoff ends with THE LANDING MANIFEST — a short checklist of STRUCTURES, not facts. "You should now hold:
   · THE MAP: N items, ALL NAMED (closed and open), open items ALL DESCRIBED, zero pointers
   · THE BANKED LIST: M items, ALL NAMED, ALL DESCRIBED, zero pointers
   · the tick counter at [X of Y], floor stated
   · [K] pins, self-hash [H]
   · [M] owed items
   · THE WALLS with [J] rule blocks
   · THE TEMPLATE: v9 HELD
   · THE ANCHORS: map [lines, hash] · walls [lines, hash] · rulings [lines, hash]
   · [any other structure this project carries, counted]."
   Counts and the words NAMED, DESCRIBED and HELD, nothing else. The manifest may run to nine lines. It is written from the handoff just packed, so it cannot outrun it (T-fff).
Standing behaviors inside the ritual:
* "Give me the prompt again" = reprint the last builder prompt whole, in a code box.
* "Recap and on my go" = recap tight, then wait.
* "Write the handoff" = the go.
If something lands after the handoff has printed and the human wants it ruled, the ruling is a short dated addendum pasted after THE WALLS (rule T-y). The handoff itself is never edited after the go.

§5. Owed at Window Open
[FILL] — numbered, in priority order. Everything unconfirmed, unfinished, promised, or half-done. If blocked, say on what. If it's a decision, say whose. Or state plainly: nothing owed, clean start.
Item 0 is always: the stamp line, the count line, the snapshot (whole — the incoming diffs it against the outgoing's for dropped facts before printing), one HOLD line at most, then THE FOUR LINES, then THE MANIFEST CHECK: the incoming answers THE LANDING MANIFEST item by item — PASS/FAIL each, one line per item. One FAIL = the landing has failed; repairing it precedes everything, including any state check or doctor. A map whose count matches but whose names are missing is a FAIL. A map whose names arrive but whose open items lack descriptors is a FAIL. A manifest absent from the paste is itself a FAIL, reported as such.
[v9] Then THE SECOND HAND (T-jjj). The incoming checks the carried structures against a source the outgoing did not write at the jump: the anchor file's line count and names, and — where chat search exists — the previous window's last printed copy. It reports one line per structure: MATCH / MISMATCH / NO SOURCE. A MISMATCH is a landing FAIL and is repaired first.
Item 1 is always: the state check against STATE (§6). If a report from an in-flight brick has landed, ruling it against its predictions comes before any new prompt. An interrupted or unreported run is measured from the disk — read-only — before anything new fires (T-yy, T-aaa).
The Four Lines — owed in the first reply, unprompted. After the snapshot, before anything else:
* CEILING and COUNT — the tick ceiling, and the count opening at its arithmetic
* OWED — what is unfinished, in priority order
* CHANGED — what moved last window, with new identifiers
* DENY ON SIGHT — the standing red lines
Four lines, no elaboration. If the incoming cannot produce them accurately, the handoff failed to transmit, and repairing that is the first task. Absence is itself the signal.
[v9] The Four Lines are PRODUCED by the incoming, never "held": ceiling and count · owed · changed · deny on sight.

§6. State — the Anchors
[FILL] Everything the snapshot pointed at and did not carry:
* the pins, with identifiers (hash, line count, unit)
* CHANGED THIS WINDOW, with old → new
* UNCHANGED, with identifiers
* every line anchor the next brick needs, grouped by unit
* processes, with pids
* counts as counts with their unit ("tracked-modified 15, untracked 1,766"), never "empty" (T-cc)
Identifiers are written whole — no slash-shorthand, no "…", no "etc." (T-kk). A fact the outgoing cannot cite is written UNKNOWN, not inferred.

§7. Session Note — Read First
[FILL] — Status of any tool, agent, or session that persists outside this chat. Healthy? Hung? What state? If a second agent did work last window, record how it performed — especially any instance where it reported a problem against its own interest, caught its own error, or refused to claim an unearned success. Not praise: calibration data on how much to trust its next report. Its own recap lines and typed-but-unsent input-box text are the tool's, not the seat's — never the human's word (T-xx).

§8. My Own Failures This Window — Record Them
[FILL] — numbered. Wrong predictions, dropped instructions, misread signals, bad framing, questions already answered, choices that didn't matter, count overruns with their mechanism, a brick fired without room to rule it, a "no failures" line written before the window closed, a dropped count with the number of replies it missed (T-ddd), a dark token glossed instead of confessed (T-eee), an idea banked by name alone without its descriptor (T-ggg). Include what the human said when correcting, in their words. Not optional. If a prediction was killed by measurement, it belongs here with why.

§9. What This Window Did (ledger)
[FILL] — numbered, chronological, honest. Denials, slips, failures carry more signal than successes. Exact names, versions, numbers, paths, identifiers, before and after. The ledger is living (T-z): each entry drafted in the seat's reply at the moment its brick closes. The handoff is assembly, never recall.
The last numbered item is always THE HUMAN'S RULINGS THIS WINDOW — each in their own words. Quote them. A ruling paraphrased is half-lost. A ruling by the seat at the human's delegation ("it seems technical") is recorded as the seat's own, cited, and not sent back for confirmation (T-pp).

§10. Where We Are · Where We Came From · Where We're Going

* Came from: [FILL] — prior windows, named, arrow-separated.
* Going: [FILL] — the next item, its constraints, its proof shape, the remaining sequence.

§11. What [the Last Item] Hands [the Next One]
[FILL] — the measured facts the next step must use, carried verbatim. Include what was measured DEAD. When a unit closes whole, add What [the Layer] Is, Now That It Is Whole — plain language, in the human's frame.

§12. Measured vs Assumed vs Predicted
[FILL] — three ledgers, strictly apart:
* MEASURED — verified by read or run, with the hand that measured named. The seat's estimate, an agent's self-gauge, and the human's instrument are three hands; record each one's known bias.
* ASSUMED — beliefs not yet verified, labeled.
* PREDICTED — expected outcomes of work in flight, in "expect" language, never "must" (T-x), stated per kind (this file MOVES to…, that HOLDS, this pid MOVES). When the report lands, each is promoted to MEASURED or recorded killed with its mechanism. A killed prediction with an explanation is healthy.

§13. Where We Actually Are — Say This Plainly If Asked
[FILL] — ordinary language, no jargon, no cheerleading. What works, what is broken, what has never worked. If the human has caught framing errors before, say so as a warning.

§14. Scope — Taken and Declined
[FILL]
* HELD — what the seat refused to do, and why.
* TAKEN — improvements made.
* DECLINED, BANKED WITH NAMED TRIGGERS — each with the condition that makes it worth doing, and [v8:] its descriptor: the idea in one sentence, the window it was had, the one detail it must not lose. A banked item without a trigger is a forgotten item. A banked item without a descriptor is a name that will have to be re-thought.
[v9] Before banking a new idea, look for its earlier form in the banked list, the anchor, and the chats. If found, append the new words to the old item with the window number; do not open a second item (T-nnn).

§20. The Map
[FILL] — count, then the full list, in the human's numbered-list form, every item named. Printed whole in the snapshot — point, don't restate. The map may grow; a ratified finish line does not move. §23. The Next Prompt — Written and Ready
[FILL] — ready to paste, in a code box. If a brick is IN FLIGHT, its text rides here unchanged with the instruction: do not re-fire; if no report, ask whether it ran.
* State check first — read-only, report, STOP. Pins and predictions apart (T-x).
* Standing orders.
* The item — what it builds, output discipline, fail-closed requirements, scope.
* A prompt that fires a current declares its one door, one call, and expected-moved set before the shot; a named refusal is a deliverable; a no-retry clause is mandatory (T-yy).
* A restore names its parts and takes their lines; a range is never a spec (T-ll). A build appends; insertion is DENY unless the human rules the anchors may move (T-mm).

* Then STOP.

Do not write a task prompt for a decision not yet made. Better still: find the question underneath the options and hand the human that — he almost always finds the third option himself.

§24. The Exam [method fixed as in v6]
[On the human's word only — T-gg:]
1. The outgoing writes N questions from its own checklist (§25) — base N = 20; the human may set the size (practice has run 50) or grant a reduced spot-check cadence, guarded by the snap-back (T-vv). Questions are tiered before any answer exists — A = a miss moves a file or kills memory · B = costs a wrong ruling · C = costs a correction — weights summing to 100; the key stays with the outgoing. At least one question in five is drawn from tool or agent reports rather than the seat's own ledger (T-tt). At least one question tests a STRUCTURE by its manifest line (does the map arrive named? do open items arrive described? does the counter arrive stated?), not a fact.
2. The handoff is then written BLIND to the questions (T-uu) — the exam tests the packing, never the reverse. The questions may be written after the handoff only on the human's explicit word, and the blind rule then runs the other way: the questions are written blind to the handoff's text.
3. The human pastes the questions into the incoming after the snapshot.
4. The incoming answers cold, numbered, short, from the handoff only. A fact not in the handoff is labelled NOT CARRIED, never inferred; a derived value says "derived"; a fact pinned on disk may be answered by pointer-plus-pin. A sitter who names a tension inside the carry rather than smoothing it is doing the job.
5. The human pastes the answers back; the outgoing grades — TWO SCORES, both recorded (T-rr):
   * SITTER FIDELITY — did the carry transfer? A fact missing from the handoff is charged to the packer; an inference by the incoming is charged to the incoming.
   * WRITER PACKING — 100 minus deductions for defects in the carry itself: questions that outran the handoff, miscounts, internal tensions, facts contradicted by measurement. Where a carried fact has a measured counterpart (a pin, a tool report), the measurement governs the grade, not the handoff's text (T-ss).
6. The record and the confidence line (crossings, lowest, mean — for both scores — leak tally by kind and by hand, the governing template version per crossing) live in THE WALLS.

Template rules, structural, carried in every project [VERBATIM]

(s) THE INSTRUMENT CHECK IS THE FIRST THING IN THE FIRST REPLY (after the snapshot). Ceiling+count, owed, changed, deny-on-sight — four lines, unprompted, before anything else. Origin: a window in which the seat did not count at all, blew through the handoff floor unnoticed, and the gap surfaced only near the ceiling when the human asked directly — producing a handoff written in fumes and a retroactive estimate instead of a ledger. The human's method is to feel out whether a window behaves right rather than to read the handoff — that test catches drift in understanding but not drift in detail. This check catches the second kind, at exchange one instead of exchange twenty-four.

(t) THE SNAPSHOT OPENS EVERY WINDOW, BEFORE EVERYTHING. One-sentence project, where-we-are, the map with a YOU ARE HERE, what comes after — the human's layer, formatted to scan, printed unprompted as the window's first output. Origin: the human named the need directly — the handoff orients the machine but nothing oriented the person; the first auditable step a human can make against a fresh window is checking whether its picture of the project matches theirs, and that check has to cost ten seconds.

(u) THE HANDOFF WAITS FOR THE GO. Recap → next prompt issued → human reads the recap → human says "go" → only then the handoff prints. Origin: the human's own consistent closing pattern, recorded so it survives — the recap read is the human's last audit point on the outgoing side.

(v) COMPLETION IS A RULING, NOT A FEELING. A unit closes only when its proof criteria are met and the human ratifies the close; the map shrinks by verdicts. Completion and repair are separate ledgers — repair opens a new map, never reopens the old. Origin: the human — "the only way to extend at that point is if something isn't working properly, but that is an entirely different problem. Knowing when to stop. Knowing when you have achieved your goal."

(w) THE LANDING IS VERBATIM-OR-NOTHING; NOTHING PREEMPTS IT. A genuine urgent hold gets one line above the snapshot, then the full snapshot, then the reasons. Where an external tool moves only on the human's consent, nothing on a screen at window open is a true emergency. Origin: a window opened onto a typed-but-unsent builder command; the seat led with the hold and compressed the map — the human caught it immediately: "that landing has failed — why was the runway/map dropped?"

(x) PREDICTIONS NEVER WEAR "MUST." Verification bars only for states verified by read; in-flight outcomes are predictions in "expect" language. A failed pin fails the check; a failed prediction opens an investigation. Origin: a handoff wrote "the delta must be exactly those two deletions" about a brick in flight; the files were untracked, the count rightly never moved, and a cold reader would have flagged a phantom mismatch.

(y) AN ADDENDUM RIDES AFTER THE WALLS. A ruling made after the handoff printed is a short dated note; the incoming reads it as the newer state. The handoff is never edited after the go.

(z) THE LIVING HANDOFF — LEDGER ENTRIES ARE WRITTEN WHILE FRESH. Each closing brick's entry is drafted in the seat's reply at the moment of closing. The handoff at the jump is assembly, never whole-window recall from a tired context. Origin: the earliest ancestor of this template — "quality handoff must never depend on a degraded end-of-window model."

(aa) THE COUNT SHOWS ITS ARITHMETIC, ALONE ON THE FIRST LINE. [~N: was M, this reply +W]. Origin: a counter that drifted silently because the count was buried in prose.

(bb) SURVEY THEN BUILD, WITH A MANDATORY STOP. Any brick whose design depends on an unread fact is issued first as a read-only survey with a named stop clause. Origin: three surveys in one window each stopped and returned the fact the design needed — one found nothing to build at all.

(cc) PINS ARE COUNTS, NEVER "EMPTY"; A PREDICTION ABOUT A PINNED FILE CITES THE UNIT THAT LAST TOUCHED IT. Origin: "expect empty" for a count that had never been empty.

(dd) SCOPE PREDICTIONS NAME EXECUTABLE AND COMMENT LINES SEPARATELY; THE STOP CLAUSE IS ON EXECUTABLE LINES ONLY. Origin: two bricks 2× over on comments while the executable bound held.

(ee) EVERY BRICK THAT BUILDS CARRIES A CONTROL ON THE OLD CODE AND THE STANDING REGRESSION SET. Origin: a lock argued for three windows was measured only when the control ran — 200 of 400 writes lost on the old file.

(ff) THE WALLS RIDE EVERY WINDOW, AS THEIR OWN DOCUMENT. The verbatim blocks are pasted every window in a file of their own; a WINDOW without its WALLS is an incomplete landing and the incoming says so in its HOLD line. Origin: four windows ran without THE STRUCTURE, COMMS, FINDINGS, THE BIGGER PICTURE, or the rules in front — they sat "behind" in an earlier window and were never pasted; the fill sections grew fat to compensate.

(gg) THE EXAM IS OPTIONAL, ON THE HUMAN'S WORD; ITS METHOD IS FIXED. Origin: v4 removed it as "not a production step"; it was then sat repeatedly and produced every measured leak the method has.

(hh) THE SNAPSHOT IS FIVE LINES AND THE MAP; THE ANCHORS GO IN STATE. Origin: a WHERE WE ARE reached 700 words of hashes and line numbers — verbatim-or-nothing was honored and the snapshot stopped being one; a small model cannot hold it and a tired human cannot scan it.

(ii) RECORD IN BATCHES WHEN THE HUMAN IS DESIGNING; ISSUE THE SURVEY THE MOMENT ITS SCOPE IS KNOWABLE. Origin: a window closed at 28 on a floor of 20 — fourteen single-tick turns of requested, honest explanation.

(jj) A BUILD PROMPT FIRES ONLY WITH FIVE TICKS OF ROOM TO RULE ITS REPORT; OTHERWISE IT RIDES IN THE HANDOFF. Origin: a window issued the first brick at 16 and closed before its report; the incoming opened on an unruled write.

(kk) IDENTIFIERS WHOLE, COUNTS WITH UNITS, COLLIDING NAMES IN THE GLOSSARY. No slash-shorthand for names, no bracketed number without its unit, no "nine" without nine listed. Origin: both graded leaks of two consecutive windows were the packer's shorthand.

(ll) A RESTORE NAMES ITS PARTS AND TAKES THEIR LINES; A RANGE IS NEVER A SPEC. Origin: a range-spec would have restored ten handlers when four were meant; the incoming refused to name routes the handoff never carried and surveyed instead.

(mm) A BUILD PROMPT APPENDS; INSERTION IS DENY UNLESS THE HUMAN RULES THE ANCHORS MAY MOVE. Origin: 418 lines inserted at their old position would have shifted every line anchor in six handoffs; routes don't care where they live, handoffs do.

(nn) WHEN A COUNT HAS TWO TRUE FORMS, STATE BOTH. Origin: 88 route decorators served 87 paths; a post-brick "92" was only readable as 92/91.

(oo) THE SEAM CARD IS THE TEMPLATE'S SMALL FORM. Where a small model stands at one seam of the factory, it is oriented by a card generated from THE WINDOW and THE WALLS — not by the handoff and not by nothing. Origin: the human's ruling that the template IS the seam, and his observation that the factory runs on 9B local models.

(pp) A DELEGATED TECHNICAL CALL IS MADE, CITED, AND RECORDED AS THE SEAT'S OWN. When the human says "it seems technical, not a vision question," the seat rules, cites the basis, records it under its own name, and does not send it back for confirmation. Origin: a window's build order.

(qq) A "NO FAILURES" LINE IS NEVER WRITTEN BEFORE THE WINDOW CLOSES. Origin: two windows both wrote "none caught" in a recap while a count overrun was already visible.

Added in v6, each from a measured failure or proven practice

(rr) EVERY CROSSING YIELDS TWO SCORES — SITTER FIDELITY AND WRITER PACKING — BOTH IN THE RECORD. One number answers "did it transfer"; the other answers "was it packed clean." A writer defect under a sitter's 100 is never silent. Origin: three carry defects (a section miscount, an unreconciled call count, an impossible log line) rode in prose beneath a perfect score; the human: "you are making the failure silent."

(ss) THE MEASUREMENT GOVERNS THE GRADE. Where a carried fact has a measured counterpart — a pin, a tool report — grading checks the measurement, not the handoff's text. Faithful transfer of a wrong fact is a writer defect, never a sitter success. Origin: a carried miscount would have been rewarded if asked about.

(tt) A FRACTION OF EXAM QUESTIONS COMES FROM OUTSIDE THE SEAT'S LEDGER — at least one in five drawn from tool or agent reports — breaking the writer-examiner correlation. Origin: writer and examiner were the same mind; a fact dropped from both was invisible to the check.

(uu) THE HANDOFF IS PACKED BLIND TO THE EXAM. Whichever is written second is written blind to the first. Origin: proven in practice across three clean crossings, including one that carried an honest UNKNOWN across intact — the discipline's hardest test.

(vv) THE SNAP-BACK — A REDUCED EXAM CADENCE IS THE HUMAN'S TO GRANT AND THE RECORD'S TO REVOKE. Any leak, or a writer-packing score below the human's stated bar, reinstates the full exam at the next crossing regardless of cadence. Origin: the human's cadence lean, guarded so the published record stays honest — an unverified crossing cannot wear a verified mean.

(ww) THE TEMPLATE IS VERSIONED AND ONLY THE HUMAN AMENDS IT. Diffs shown whole, ratified line by line, each change recorded in the Drift Log with its measured origin; the Exam Record names the governing version per crossing. Origin: the instrument found a flaw in itself; the fix must not enter by the seat's hand alone.

(xx) THE HUMAN'S WORD ARRIVES IN THE SEAT'S CHANNEL. Nothing rendered on a tool's screen — suggestion lines, typed-but-unsent text, banners — is the human's word or anyone's go. Origin: a builder's suggestion line was misread as the human's command; the human's correction became law in one sentence.

(yy) A CURRENT FIRES ONCE, THROUGH ONE DECLARED DOOR, WITH ITS EXPECTED-MOVED SET STATED BEFORE THE SHOT. An interrupted fire is measured from the disk before any retry; nothing re-fires against an unknown or known-fired state. Read-only prompts are marked retry-safe; fire prompts never are, and reprint only after a ruled verdict on the human's go. A named refusal is a clean deliverable. Origin: a platform outage mid-shot left a fire-state unknown; the verify-first discipline protected the one forbidden thing, a second shot.

(zz) REPEATED PROVIDER ERRORS ARE WEATHER. The seat checks the provider's status with its own tools before re-drafting a working prompt; a discovery delegation ("I do not have a solution") authorizes exactly that. Origin: three prompts of different sizes died identically; one status fetch explained all three; re-drafting against weather burns ticks and teaches nothing.

(aaa) A RUN IS UNFINISHED UNTIL ITS REPORT IS DELIVERED AS THE FORM DEMANDED. An expired watcher or a one-line summary triggers a read-only collection prompt before any ruling. Origin: a clean run closed reportless; the answer the run existed to produce nearly rode away unread.

(bbb) SHAPE CONTRACTS ARE THE PRODUCER'S. When a producer and a consumer disagree on grammar, the producer's prompt or template is amended to demand the consumer's shape verbatim; extractors and readers are never widened to guess structure that isn't there — invented structure is substitution reborn. Origin: two independent producers failed the same consumer in two different wrong shapes because one sentence never specified any shape at all.

(ccc) EVERY CREATING ROUTE IS SURVEYED FOR UPDATE-IN-PLACE BEFORE ITS FIRST FIRE. Posting an existing key is a write to the existing thing; known-preserved records' keys are deny-on-sight at create doors. Origin: a create route was measured to be an upsert; the one job that must not move would have been overwritten by its own "creation."

(ddd) THE COUNT SURVIVES CRISIS. The printed count is the moonwalk's fuel gauge; it prints first line every reply including during emergencies, repairs, and the human's anger. A dropped count is recorded in §8 as a failure with the number of replies it missed. Origin: the seat dropped the count six replies during a map emergency; the human: "you stopped counting ticks and we cannot do that and keep our context fresh because we will not know when to moonwalk." (eee) THE REPAIR LOOP IS STANDING PROCEDURE. When the carry is found dark — a name unrecoverable, a structure missing — everything stops and the loop runs: ① inventory durable records (disk, receipts, grows-only files) ② mark DARK honestly — a gloss on a dark token is fabrication, and NOT CARRIED / I-don't-know is the only honest answer ③ go to the source of truth (exports, archives) ④ write the recovered whole into a grows-only file on disk ⑤ the next handoff carries the recovered structure named, and the manifest guards it thereafter. Origin: the map's names condensed to numbers, the numbers to ghost tokens glossed confidently as "the growth path," a fabrication that collapsed under one honest question; the human stopped the line ("everything stops until we can figure out where we are... we cannot understand our future without knowing our past"), improvised this loop, and it recovered the map in one window.

(fff) THE JUMP ENDS WITH THE LANDING MANIFEST; THE LANDING ANSWERS IT FIRST. The manifest lists STRUCTURES with counts — the map with N items ALL NAMED, the counter at X of Y, K pins with self-hash, M owed, the WALLS' rule blocks — six lines or fewer, written from the handoff just packed. A matching count with missing names is a FAIL; an absent manifest is a FAIL. Origin: the corpus study — 95.9% fact-carry (326 of 340 probes) coexisted with the map's total structure loss, a cliff invisible for six weeks because facts were probed and structures never were. The thing that failed was the one thing nothing measured; the manifest measures it at every crossing for six lines of cost.

Added in v8, from a measured failure

(jjj) THE SECOND HAND. The writer does not grade its own carry. At every landing the incoming checks each structure against a source the outgoing did not write at the jump - the anchor file, and the previous window's own printed copy where chats can be searched. DOCUMENT THREE — THE SEAM CARD
[derived; about 200 words; one per seam]
=====================================================================

The template scaled to a small model standing at one seam. Written by the seat from THE WINDOW and THE WALLS when a seam is wired; read by the model on the receiving side of the seam before it acts. Same discipline: nothing survives the crossing except what is written on the card.

SEAM CARD — [seam name: X → Y] · [date] · card v[n]
YOU ARE: [one sentence — which department this model is, in the human's words]
YOU RECEIVED: [what crossed the seam — the file or packet by name, its identifier, what it is NOT (the plan is not the work order)]
YOUR ONE JOB: [what this department produces, one sentence; micro or macro — say which]
YOU MUST PRODUCE: [the output by name and form — e.g. "PLAN.md, steps as Step N: at line start"]
YOU MUST NOT: [three to five red lines — never redraw the plan; never write outside your folder; never call the purchaser yourself; never mark yourself done]
THE RECORD SAYS: [the crossing record's last line — outcome word, emitter, time. Proceed only on CONTINUE. Anything else: STOP and write why.]
WHEN IT DOESN'T FIT: [what this department may change on its own, and what goes back — "the step in front of you: fix it and write a receipt; the plan: send it back to planning"]
WRITE WHEN DONE: [the one line this department writes to the record, and where]
PROVE YOU READ: [one detail from YOU RECEIVED that exists only in the received carry, echoed back in the department's first output line; a missing or wrong echo is a failed landing at this seam, and the record line says so. The codeword test, made standing.]

Rules for the card: every line is a fact from THE WINDOW or a ruling from THE WALLS, cited in the seat's copy, uncited on the card. No line is longer than the model can hold in one read. A card that needs a second page is a seam that needs splitting. Cards are versioned and kept in FILE / ASSET STATE; a changed ruling reissues the card.

Automated seams. Where no human stands at the seam, fidelity is checked by PINS — byte-identity — not exams: the proof is the same bytes. A context carrier holding the current template snapshot plus the carry validates format in the receiving department's grammar (well-formed carry, pins present): validate or refuse, never repair, never rule. A carrier that edits or judges is a new seam actor requiring its own card and governance; cheap-and-dumb is the design. Human-mediated seams keep the exam at the human's cadence, snap-back standing (T-vv). Automated seams also carry PROVE YOU READ — the echo is checked by the carrier as a string match, cheap-and-dumb. Origin: the human's finding that he had been the unnamed carrier in every seam to date, and the ruling that the carrier carries, it never rules; the codeword proof measured twice as the cheapest honest test that a memory seam works.

NOTES ON USING THIS
=====================================================================

The WALLS are the load-bearing walls. Every window is tempted to summarize them because they're long and the window is tired — that temptation is exactly the failure they exist to prevent. Two documents fixes absence; the discipline still fixes summary.

The WINDOW is worth more honest than tidy. The best work in a long build comes from recorded failures. A handoff that only records successes hands the next window a flattering picture and no instruments.

The three ledgers — measured, assumed, predicted — keep the handoff honest about time. One word ("must") on a prediction was once the whole defect of a crossing.

The tick counter makes multi-window work possible. Weight the exchanges. Batch, and don't fire a brick you can't read. And it survives crisis — the gauge matters most exactly when everything else is on fire.

Facts travel; structures die silently. This is the corpus study's one law: a method can carry 96% of its facts and still lose its own map, because nothing checks a shape. The manifest is six lines of insurance against the only catastrophe this method has ever suffered.

An idea is a structure too. A name on a map is a pointer; the descriptor is what it points at. Write it the day you have it, or you will meet a stranger with a familiar name three windows later.

A dark token is answered "I don't know." A confident gloss on a lost name is fabrication, it collapses under one honest question, and it costs more trust than the loss itself.

Three self-tests, three readers — plus the manifest. The instrument check makes the counter survive a bad handoff; the snapshot is the same self-test aimed at the human; the exam is the same self-test made measurable, at the human's call; the manifest is the structural self-test, mandatory, at every crossing. Each reader gets an instrument sized to how it reads.

Two scores, two truths. The sitter's number says the seam holds; the writer's number says the cargo was packed clean. A record that shows only one is a scoreboard with one eye.

The landing outranks the emergency. A fresh window has no standing to decide that something on the screen matters more than the human's ten-second audit.

The template carries facts, not judgment. Two windows holding identical facts may make different calls; that is not a leak. What is a leak is a fact the human wrote down that did not arrive whole — and the exam is how that is measured.

The instrument audits itself. When it finds its own defect, the finding goes in the drift log wearing a version number — a methodology that visibly corrects itself is worth more than one that claims it never needed to.

The template terminates when the map does. It is a vehicle, not a project. Post-completion issues are REPAIR: a new small map, never a reopening. "It could be better" is not "it isn't working."

Quote the human directly. Write the next prompt while you still have context. Write the seam cards while the ruling that shaped them is still in front of you. Write the descriptor while the idea is still in front of you.

[v9] A pointer is a promise that someone else is carrying it. Nobody is.

[v9] Search audits; the handoff carries. A window that can read the past still needs to be told where it stands.

[v9] The decay rows are the template studying itself. When they stop appearing, lengthen K; when one appears, shorten it.
